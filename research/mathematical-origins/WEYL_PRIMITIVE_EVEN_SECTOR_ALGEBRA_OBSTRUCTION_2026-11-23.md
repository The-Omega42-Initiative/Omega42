# Weyl orbit-sum obstruction: primitive parity-even sector is not a subalgebra

**Independent mathematical audit, proposal only — 23 November 2026.**

This settles a specific open question raised in SPECTRAL_COMPATIBILITY_AND_OBSTRUCTION_2026-11-23.md. It does not claim that no alternative embedding or algebra structure exists.

Let N=3^k, k>=1, and T=A²=[[1,1],[1,2]] modulo N, with det(T)=1. Use a standard finite Weyl system {W_x:x in (Z/N)^2} on C^N with W_0=I, W_x W_y=ζ^(inv2·ω(x,y)) W_(x+y), where ζ=exp(2πi/N), inv2 is the inverse of 2 modulo N and ω(x,y)=x_1 y_2−x_2 y_1. Thus W_x^*=W_(-x) and Tr(W_x)=0 for x≠0, while Tr(I)=N. Weyl operators form an orthogonal Hilbert–Schmidt basis. This phase convention is appropriate for odd N; the argument below uses only W_xW_(-x)=I and trace orthogonality.

For each T-orbit O, define its orbit sum S_O=Σ_(x∈O) W_x. Since T is symplectic, the metaplectic cat-map conjugation permutes W_x as W_(Tx) in the usual exact Egorov convention, so S_O lies in the cat-map commutant. The MF-185 orbit-sum basis hypothesis is taken as the framework being audited.

## Lemma — antipodal closure

Every primitive T-orbit O is invariant under x↦−x. Indeed, the Fibonacci period theorem gives A^(4·3^(k−1))=−I modulo 3^k, so T^(2·3^(k−1))=−I. Therefore −x belongs to the same T-orbit as x.

## Theorem — explicit failure of multiplicative closure

Let V_prim be the linear span of S_O over primitive T-orbits O. Let V_even be the subspace spanned by the parity-even combinations S_(O_q)+S_(O_(-q)), where O_q is the primitive T-orbit with Q=q (MF-184). Then neither V_prim nor V_even is closed under operator multiplication.

**Proof.** Every element of V_prim is traceless, because each W_x in each primitive orbit has x≠0. Take any primitive orbit O. The coefficient of W_0=I in S_O² is

\[
\sum_{x,y\in O:x+y=0}\zeta^{\mathrm{inv2}\,\omega(x,y)}
=\sum_{x\in O}1=|O|>0,
\]

because −O=O and ω(x,−x)=0. By Weyl orthogonality, Tr(S_O²)=N|O|>0, so S_O² cannot lie in V_prim.

For the parity-even sector, set E_q=S_(O_q)+S_(O_(-q)), q a unit modulo N. The two T-orbits are disjoint, and their union is antipodally invariant. Thus E_q is a sum over 2|O_q| distinct Weyl operators, all nonzero, and the coefficient of I in E_q² is 2|O_q|=8·3^(k−1)>0. Hence Tr(E_q²)>0 although every element of V_even has trace zero. Therefore E_q² is not in V_even. QED.

## Exact Hilbert–Schmidt Gram matrix

For orbit sums over disjoint T-orbits, orthogonality of the Weyl basis gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(S_O^*S_P)=N\,|O|\,\delta_{O,P}.
\]

Thus **the Weyl orbit sums are orthogonal** (contrary to the earlier caution that orthogonality was not yet asserted), but are not normalized unless divided by sqrt(N|O|). For primitive orbits, |O|=4·3^(k−1). For parity-even pair sums E_q, Tr(E_q^*E_p)=2N|O| δ_[q],[p] after selecting one representative of each ± norm pair. This provides an explicit isometric linear embedding of the orbit-label Hilbert space into the commutant after normalization, but **not an algebra homomorphism from pointwise multiplication**.

## Important scope and convention notes

The obstruction is specifically to the **linear primitive parity-even span being an operator subalgebra**. It does not exclude a nonunital Jordan/module structure, a larger algebra generated by that span, or a different nonlinear or algebraic embedding. Nor does it contradict MF-185's statement that the *full* commutant is an algebra.

The formula for S_O in the cat-map commutant assumes an exact Egorov action with no orbit-dependent phase; confirm the precise finite quantum cat-map convention used by the canonical manuscript before claiming full manuscript parity. The algebraic identity-coefficient argument is valid for the standard Weyl system regardless of the choice of symplectic phase convention, provided W_xW_(-x)=I.

The companion exact integer test independently enumerates primitive T-orbits for k=1..4 and verifies the antipodal and identity-coefficient counts. It is finite corroboration, not the all-depth proof.
