# Independent algebraic audit of MF-43 layer lifting

**Proposal-only, 23 November 2026.** Scope: independently reconstruct the key group-generation argument behind the finite depth-r shear theorem. The existing implementation is tools/fsg.py, checks F.7–F.9 and D.3. No changes to canonical statements.

Let \(A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\), \(B_r=I+3^r E_{21}\), and \(G_{k,r}=\langle A,B_r\rangle\bmod3^k\), with \(1\le r\le k\).

## Lemma 1 — exact first-layer generators

For i=0,1,2,3, set \(D_i=A^iB_rA^{-i}=I+3^r C_i\) over the integers, with \(C_i=A^iE_{21}A^{-i}\). Reducing \(C_i\) modulo 3 gives

\[
C_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
C_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\2&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
C_2=\begin{pmatrix}2&2\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
C_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first three already span \(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_3)\), the 3-dimensional vector space of trace-zero matrices. Indeed \(C_0=E_{21}\), \(C_3=E_{12}\), and \(C_1-C_0-C_3=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\), whose diagonal is nonzero; thus the four span the diagonal trace-zero direction and both off-diagonal directions. (The diagonal calculation is not needed for independence: C_1 has nonzero diagonal and C_0,C_3 do not.)

## Lemma 2 — lifting from level n to n+1

For any n>=r and any integer matrix C, \((I+3^n C)^3\equiv I+3^{n+1}C\pmod{3^{n+2}}\). This follows from the binomial expansion: the quadratic term is \(3^{2n+1}C^2\), divisible by \(3^{n+2}\) for n>=1; the cubic term is \(3^{3n}C^3\), divisible by \(3^{n+2}\) for n>=1 except n=1, where 3n=n+2 and it is still divisible.

Consequently, for every n>=r,

\[
D_i^{3^{n-r}}\equiv I+3^n C_i\pmod{3^{n+1}}.
\]

Their products and inverses therefore generate the entire trace-zero congruence layer \(I+3^n\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_3)\) modulo \(3^{n+1}\). For determinant-one matrices in the principal congruence subgroup, the leading layer must have trace zero because \(\det(I+3^nX)\equiv1+3^n\operatorname{tr}X\pmod{3^{n+1}}\).

## Theorem — complete principal determinant-one kernel

Let \(H_{k,r}=\{M\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/3^k):M\equiv I\pmod{3^r}\}\). Then

\[
\boxed{H_{k,r}=\langle A^iB_rA^{-i}:i=0,1,2,3\rangle\quad\bmod3^k}
\]

for every k>=r. In particular \(|H_{k,r}|=3^{3(k-r)}\).

**Proof.** At level r both sides reduce to the identity. Suppose the generated subgroup surjects onto \(H_{n,r}\). For any \(M\in H_{n+1,r}\), choose a word W in the D_i whose reduction modulo 3^n equals that of M. Then \(MW^{-1}\equiv I\pmod{3^n}\) and \(\det(MW^{-1})=1\). By Lemma 2, the D_i generate all possible trace-zero layer corrections modulo 3^(n+1). Multiply W by the corresponding correction to represent M modulo 3^(n+1). Induct to k. Each layer has 3^3 elements, giving the cardinality. QED.

## Consequence for MF-43's group order

Reduction of \(G_{k,r}\) modulo \(3^r\) is the cyclic group generated by A, of order \(8\cdot3^{r-1}\) (the established Fibonacci order theorem). Its kernel contains H_(k,r) by the theorem above. Conversely, every element of G_(k,r) has determinant \(\pm1\) and reduction modulo \(3^r\) in \(\langle A\rangle\); an element reducing to identity modulo \(3^r\) must have determinant 1 because \(-1\not\equiv1\pmod{3^r}\). Thus the kernel is exactly H_(k,r). Hence

\[
|G_{k,r}|=(8\cdot3^{r-1})(3^{3(k-r)})
=\boxed{8\cdot3^{3k-2r-1}}.
\]

This independently reconstructs the **group-order half** of MF-43 from elementary matrix calculations and the already established Fibonacci order theorem.

## Scope of independence and remaining gap

The argument independently supplies the all-depth group-generation and order proof; it does **not** independently establish that the complete orbit invariant is (j,Q(u)^2 mod 3^min(r,k-j)). The latter still needs an orbit-stabilizer/transitivity argument. One route is to prove SL2 principal-congruence transitivity on primitive vectors with fixed reduction modulo 3^r, then combine the Fibonacci orbit classification at depth r. That route is the next audit step. No numerical check or finite search is presented as an all-depth proof.
