# O42-MOR-01: Mathematical Origins of Reality

## Central question

Can the fundamental features of existence be understood, represented or derived from mathematical structures and their transformations?

## Four propositions kept apart

| Proposition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mathematical existence | A structure is well defined and satisfies its axioms |
| Physical existence | Something exists as part of the physical universe |
| Ontological identity | Physical reality is that structure, not something represented by it |
| Generative origin | The structure explains how existence, change and complexity arise |

That the Omega42 law has 81 states does not show that the physical world has 81 fundamental states. A representation is not an origin.

## Predecessors

- The Mathematical Universe Hypothesis (Tegmark, 2007): physical reality is a mathematical structure. Criticized for assuming what it argues and for its testability.
- Ontic structural realism (Ladyman, French and others): structure and relations are more fundamental than objects. The meaning of "structure" is disputed.
- Digital physics and "it from bit" (Wheeler): information as the ground of physical reality.
- Mathematical Platonism and structuralism: whether structures exist apart from what instantiates them.

## Three instruments

1. **The relationship register** (records/relationships/register.json): every relationship is one of seven kinds: analogy, structural correspondence, mathematical equivalence, derivation, physical realization, empirical correlation, causal relationship.
2. **The comparison program**: the construct beside Fibonacci recurrences, finite fields and modular arithmetic, graphs and networks, dynamical systems and attractors, symmetry groups and invariants, cellular automata, information-theoretic structures and quantum state spaces, to find what is distinctive and what is already known.
3. **The challenge program**: mathematicians, physicists, philosophers of science and consciousness researchers are invited to name a result, relationship, derivation or prediction that would not have been obtained without the construct.

## The test above the others

Can the construct derive something not built into its starting assumptions? A structure that returns what was encoded in it is a representation. A result nobody anticipated that survives independent scrutiny is a discovery. A full derivation would define the structure apart from the desired result, derive its states and transformations, identify conditions for stability and interaction, represent known structures, recover established results in their limits and give at least one discriminating prediction.
