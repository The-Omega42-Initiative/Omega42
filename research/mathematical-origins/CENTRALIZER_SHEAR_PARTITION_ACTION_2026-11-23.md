# Centralizer action on the depth-r shear orbit partitions

**Proposal-only mathematics — 23 November 2026.** An explicit bridge between the centralizer theorem and the shear refinement theorem. No changes to canonical material.

Let R_k=Z/3^k, A=[[0,1],[1,1]], B_r=[[1,0],[3^r,1]], C=I+3A=[[1,3],[3,4]], and Q(a,b)=a²+ab−b². For 1<=r<=k, let P_(k,r) be the partition of R_k² into orbits under G_(k,r)=<A,B_r>. Let x=3^j u be a nonzero state, n=k-j, h=min(r,n), and write L_r(x)=Q(u)² mod 3^h.

## Theorem — cyclic permutation of all shear orbits within each shell

1. The linear operator C sends every G_(k,r)-orbit **bijectively onto another G_(k,r)-orbit**, even though C need not normalize the group G_(k,r).
2. On shell j, C induces the permutation L -> 25 L modulo 3^h of the 3^(h-1) possible unit-square labels. This permutation is **one cycle of exact length 3^(h-1)**.
3. Consequently, each shear orbit is the union of exactly 3^(n-h) consecutive primitive Fibonacci orbits under the rotation by C^(3^(h-1)). More precisely, the set of primitive Fibonacci orbit labels within a given shear orbit is one congruence class in the cyclic orbit-index group Z/3^(n-1) modulo 3^(h-1), and contains 3^(n-h) labels.
4. The ternary refinement from shear depth r to r+1 (when r<n) is the reduction map of cyclic index groups Z/3^r -> Z/3^(r-1). The three child shear orbits correspond to the three lifts of a parent label.

## Proof

C is invertible and commutes with A. Its norm multiplier is Q(Cu)=−5Q(u), so the squared-norm label transforms as L_r(Cx)=25L_r(x) mod 3^h. The complete orbit classification, independently reconstructed in the MF-43 audit, identifies G_(k,r)-orbits with the fibers of L_r within each valuation shell. Since multiplication by 25 is a bijection on the unit-square residues, C maps each fiber bijectively onto the corresponding new fiber. This proves (1), without requiring C to conjugate G_(k,r) to itself.

The unit-square group modulo 3^h is cyclic of order 3^(h-1). Since 25=1+24 and v3(24)=1, the lifting-the-exponent identity gives v3(25^(3^t)-1)=t+1, so 25 generates the group of unit squares. Thus C cycles through every label once, proving (2).

At the finer Fibonacci depth n, the primitive A-orbit labels are the unit squares modulo 3^n, and C cycles through them by multiplication by 25. The shear orbit label is their reduction modulo 3^h. This reduction has kernel of order 3^(n-h), exactly the subgroup generated by 25^(3^(h-1)) in the cyclic group of order 3^(n-1). Hence each shear orbit is the union of 3^(n-h) Fibonacci orbits, and its index set is a coset of that kernel. This proves (3). Note that these are **arithmetic progressions of C-orbit indices**, not consecutive integers in the ordinary order; the word consecutive is used only in the sense of successive powers of the subgroup generator C^(3^(h-1)).

For r<n, the change from h=r to h=r+1 is reduction from squares modulo 3^(r+1) to squares modulo 3^r, with a kernel of order three. Under the generators 25, this is reduction of exponents modulo 3^(r-1), proving (4). QED.

## Exact example at k=4, primitive shell

There are 27 primitive Fibonacci orbits, indexed by C^t(1,0), 0<=t<27. At shear depth r=1, all 27 belong to one shear orbit. At r=2, they form three shear orbits, each with the nine indices t≡s (mod 3). At r=3, there are nine shear orbits, each with three indices t≡s (mod 9). At r=4, there are 27 singleton Fibonacci-orbit classes, one per index.

The **partition** is canonical once the base orbit is chosen; the integer labels depend on the chosen seed.

## Limits

C permutes the **sets** of shear orbits; the theorem does not assert that C normalizes G_(k,r) as a matrix subgroup. No semantic, physical, or consciousness conclusion follows. The accompanying test independently checks the orbit-label action and exact congruence classes at small levels.
