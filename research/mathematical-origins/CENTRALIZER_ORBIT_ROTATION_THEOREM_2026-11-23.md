# Centralizer orbit rotation: constructive cyclic enumeration of Fibonacci orbits

**Proposal-only mathematical theorem, 23 November 2026.** Derived from existing MF-39/MF-55, with a new explicit commuting orbit-rotation operator. No priority claim against external literature.

Let R_k=Z/(3^k), A=[[0,1],[1,1]], Q(a,b)=a²+ab−b² and C=I+3A=[[1,3],[3,4]]. Write P_k for the set of **primitive** A-orbits on R_k² (states with at least one coordinate not divisible by 3).

## Theorem

1. C is invertible over R_k and commutes with A. For every state x, Q(Cx)=−5Q(x) mod 3^k.
2. C permutes the 3^(k−1) primitive Fibonacci orbits **in one cycle of exact length 3^(k−1)**. Equivalently, C is a constructive generator of the cyclic torsor of primitive A-orbits.
3. The full invertible matrix centralizer of A is
   Z_GL2(R_k)(A)={aI+bA: a,b in R_k, a²+ab−b² is a unit}.
   Its quotient by <A> is canonically identified through the norm modulo sign with R_k^×/{±1}, a cyclic group of order 3^(k−1), generated by the class of C.
4. For a state x of coordinate valuation j<k, C permutes the A-orbits **within that shell** in a single cycle of length 3^(k−j−1). Zero is fixed.

## Proof

The identity A²=A+I implies every matrix commuting with A has form aI+bA: writing X=[[p,q],[r,s]], XA=AX gives q=r and s=p+q. Its determinant is a²+ab−b². Thus the invertible centralizer is precisely the unit group of the unramified quadratic algebra R_k[φ], where multiplication by φ acts as A. In this algebra C corresponds to 1+3φ and has norm det(C)=−5. Multiplicativity of the algebraic norm proves Q(Cx)=−5Q(x), and commutation is immediate.

MF-39 establishes that the norm map from this unit group onto R_k^× is surjective and that the inverse image of {±1} is exactly <φ>=<A>. Consequently the norm induces an isomorphism Z_GL2(R_k)(A)/<A> ≅ R_k^×/{±1}. The latter is cyclic of order 3^(k−1), since R_k^× ≅ {±1} × (1+3R_k).

It remains to show that C generates the quotient. The norm of C is −5, and −5≡1 mod 3 with v_3(−5−1)=v_3(−6)=1. The lifting-the-exponent identity (or the binomial induction) gives v_3((−5)^(3^t)−1)=t+1 for all t≥0. Hence −5 has exact multiplicative order 3^(k−1) modulo 3^k. Its class generates R_k^×/{±1}. Alternatively, on the orbit invariant Q(x)², C acts by multiplication by 25; v_3(25−1)=1, so 25 also has order 3^(k−1) and cycles all unit squares.

The primitive A-orbits are classified by Q(x)² mod 3^k (MF-55), and the possible values are precisely the 3^(k−1) unit squares. Therefore C is transitive and acts regularly on this orbit set. For shell j, dividing by 3^j reduces the action to primitive vectors modulo 3^(k−j); apply the same result at that smaller level. QED.

## Computational consequences

A single primitive seed, such as x=(1,0), produces one representative from every primitive Fibonacci orbit by x, Cx, C²x, ..., C^(3^(k−1)−1)x. The complete nonzero orbit census can be constructed by repeating this procedure on each valuation shell; no exhaustive search over all 9^k states is necessary to obtain representatives.

The companion standard-library test checks all k=1..5: matrix commutation, norm multiplier, order of 25 and −5, primitive orbit labels, and the shell-specific orbit rotation. These are finite tests, not the proof.

## Limits

This is an arithmetic symmetry of the finite norm algebra. It does not select a unique orbit, justify semantic classifications, or imply physical or consciousness dynamics. The statement that C generates a quotient depends on the existing MF-39 kernel/surjectivity result, while the orbit interpretation uses MF-55.
