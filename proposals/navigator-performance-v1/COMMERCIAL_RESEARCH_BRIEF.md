# Ω42 Data and Commercialisation Brief

Status: strategic proposal. No market valuation or customer demand has been established.

## What Ω42 can generate
Potential structured records: exact Strike and consented normalized form; coordinate and arithmetic trace; instrument selection and execution status; model/protocol versions; four-arm readings; alternate framings and coordinates; disagreements and corrections; typed OmegaSL representations; orbit/matrix/temporal outputs; provenance, hashes and review status.

Such data may support research on structured reasoning, prompt sensitivity, model disagreement, mathematical tool use, provenance, calibration and human-AI collaboration. Volume alone is not value: duplication, synthetic agreement, contamination, weak labels, model drift and selection bias can undermine a corpus.

## Research-grade data requirements
Record consent and permitted uses; de-identification; retention/deletion policy; source/protocol version; model/provider/version; prior-output exposure; exact input/transformation history; tool execution versus reconstruction; instrument version; uncertainty/review/correction history; license and commercial-use permission. Separate public open records from private contributions. Do not collect sensitive personal data by default or sell identifiable conversations. Open-source licenses do not grant rights over personal data submitted by users.

## Lab value proposition
Sell reproducible research infrastructure rather than an unfiltered text dump:
1. Frozen benchmark suite, controls and scoring rubrics.
2. Opt-in, versioned, de-identified structured corpus with clear rights.
3. Evaluation API returning structured trace and machine-readable results.
4. Model comparison with exposure/provenance tracking.
5. Joint research partnerships with preregistration and publication terms.
6. Private/on-premise deployment for confidential workflows.

Research questions include whether structured navigation improves traceability, how wording perturbations affect outputs, whether instruments catch arithmetic/frame errors, whether typed representations improve reproducibility and how stable cross-model interpretations are. These are hypotheses, not proven benefits.

## Revenue model compatible with open protocol
- Hosted Navigator workspace: per-seat subscription for managed runs, projects and collaboration.
- Batch/API: metered structured navigation.
- Private deployment: annual contract plus integration/support.
- Benchmark/evaluation: project fee or annual access.
- Curated corpus: licensed access or funded collaboration, only with valid consent and rights.
- Training: institutional cohorts and instrument operation.
- Sponsored research: grants, university/lab contracts.
- Implementation: integration and support services.

Keep the protocol and public tools open if that remains the project's commitment. Monetize hosted execution, integration, evaluation, support and research services. Avoid patents if the no-patents pledge remains binding. Trademark/certification claims require governance and legal review.

## Pilot
Run a 6–8 week pilot with one lab: agree research question and data-use terms; freeze protocol and benchmark; compare at least two model families if feasible; include baseline/control; log hashes and execution status; blind scoring where appropriate; report failures and negative results; deliver reproducible dataset slice, analysis and API demo. Charge only after scope, rights and deliverables are agreed.

## Illustrative pricing hypotheses (USD, not market quotations)
Individual hosted workspace $15–$40/seat/month; research team $150–$750/month; API metered per run/batch with transparent cost floor; scoped pilot $5,000–$25,000; private integration $15,000–$75,000+ depending on security and scope. These are hypotheses for customer discovery, not a valuation or forecast. Validate willingness to pay, compute cost, support, tax and procurement.

## Research fund
Proposed allocation of net service revenue: 50% protocol/instrument research; 20% infrastructure/security; 15% contributor support; 10% legal/privacy/governance; 5% reserve. This requires formal approval. Publish quarterly revenue, costs, grants, research outputs and conflicts.

## Immediate commercial actions
Audit COMMERCIAL.md, PATENT_PLEDGE.md, licenses, trademark and governance. Define data dictionary and consent model before corpus collection. Build a benchmark with controls and contamination tracking. Interview 10–15 labs/university groups about evaluation pain, privacy, procurement and budget. Secure one paid/grant pilot before expensive hosting. Publish only synthetic/demo data until consent and rights are clear. Market traceability, modular instruments and reproducibility—not unproven claims of improved reasoning.

## Risks
Privacy and proprietary inputs; copyright/licensing; scientific validity; contamination; overclaiming; inference/storage/support costs; mission drift. The strongest asset is not raw data volume but an open protocol plus deterministic instruments, typed traces, provenance, reproducible benchmarks and a trusted research process.
