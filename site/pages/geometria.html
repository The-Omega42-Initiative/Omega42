What shapes know. The Golden Torus.

Fold a square so that its opposite edges meet, and you have a torus. Stretch it by the golden ratio along one direction while squeezing it along another, and you have one of the most famous chaotic maps in mathematics. Inside it, perfectly ordered, live the 81 states of Omega42.

Constitutional Geometry · CG-02 exactThe law is the square root of Arnold's cat map

The Omega42 law is the matrix A = [[0, 1], [1, 1]]. Square it and you get [[1, 1], [1, 2]], the cat map: the classic chaotic map of the torus. So A itself acts on the whole continuous torus. On the real plane it stretches by φ ≈ 1.618 and squeezes by 1/φ, keeping area, flipping orientation each step. Almost every point of the torus wanders without ever repeating.

CG-02b exactThe 81 states are the ninth-points

The state (a, b) is the point (a/9, b/9) of the torus, and the law moves these points exactly as it moves the states. Every one of them comes back: one never moves, eight return after 8 steps, seventy-two after 24. Next to them, the pink point starts at an irrational position, and the same law scatters it across the whole square.

Order and chaos are the same law, seen at different points. Points with denominator 27, 81, 243 and beyond are the deeper floors of the Omega42 tower: an infinite family of ordered worlds, each three times finer, all inside one chaotic torus.

CG-02c exactHow many points come back after n steps?

A point of the torus that returns after n steps is a solution of (Aⁿ − I)x = 0 on the torus, and there are exactly |det(Aⁿ − I)| of them. For this law that number is |Lₙ − (−1)ⁿ − 1|, where Lₙ are the Lucas numbers 2, 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, … For n = 24 it is 103,680 = 81 × 1,280: the 81 states are one of the subgroups of points that return after 24 steps.

Every drawing of a 24-step loop rings the same: its frequencies cannot tell a circle from a square. The orbit census is recovered from the spectrum alone: the dynamics can be heard, the drawing cannot.
