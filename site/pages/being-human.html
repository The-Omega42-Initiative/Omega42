The rhythm of becoming

Every state of the Omega42 world returns. Nothing is lost, and nothing is repeated without passing through everything else first. Halfway around each long loop, at the twelfth step, every state meets its own negative, and twelve steps later it is home again.

Growth has this shape more often than we admit. The habit we thought we had left behind comes back; the question we answered at twenty asks itself again at forty. The return is not a failure. Halfway through any long change we meet our opposite, and the journey is not complete until we have passed through it.

The grace of limitation

Each state of the Omega42 world carries a quantity it cannot change. The form Q flips its sign at every step, and its square never moves. That one fixed value is what makes each loop a loop of its own, distinct from the others.

A limit is often what makes an identity possible. A sonnet has fourteen lines; a life has one body and a finite span. What we cannot change about ourselves is also what makes us recognizably ourselves through every change we do make. Creativity starts where a boundary is accepted and then explored.

The geometry of relationships

Two states of the Omega42 world can be coupled. The coupling depends on a quantity that belongs to neither state alone, the area spanned by the pair, and that quantity survives as both partners evolve. Coupling first and evolving after gives exactly the same result as evolving first and coupling after.

Some of what we are exists only between us and another person: not in either of us, but in the pair. A good relationship lets both people keep changing without losing what they share. The question is not whether we change but whether what we share is the kind of thing that survives the change.

The balance of doing and being

The canonical loop begins with 2 and 5. Modulo nine, 2 × 5 = 10 ≡ 1: multiplying by 2 and multiplying by 5 undo each other. One can be read as making, the other as understanding, and each completes the other.

A life of only doing exhausts itself; a life of only reflecting never arrives anywhere. The rhythm between acting and comprehending what we have done may be the oldest skill a person learns, and the one that is never finished.
