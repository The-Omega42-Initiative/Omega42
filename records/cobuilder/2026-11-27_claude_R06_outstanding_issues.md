## 4. C-R3b: a polynomial upper bound, proved (Theorem 20.6)

**Theorem 20.6.** The largest {A, C, S} distance at depth k is at most c · k⁵ for a constant c. With the lower bound 2k (Proposition 20.5), the distance is polynomial in k, so polylogarithmic in the 9ᵏ states. Under {A, V, C} it is exponential (Theorem 20.4).

**Proof outline** (in full in paper §20). It combines two published theorems.
1. Λ = ⟨A, S⟩ ∩ SL₂(ℤ) is generated by S, A² and ASA⁻¹, and it is Zariski dense.
2. Bourgain and Varjú (Invent. Math. 188, 2012) prove that its Cayley graphs modulo every q are expanders. So the symmetric diameter of ⟨A, S⟩ mod 3ᵏ is O(k).
3. Babai (SODA 2006, Theorem 1.4) proves that a Cayley digraph has directed diameter O(d² (log n)³), where d is the undirected diameter. This gives positive words of length O(k⁵).
4. Transitivity on primitive states (Theorem 20.3) and the fact that C commutes with A and S finish the proof.

**Limits of this result.**
- The constant is not explicit.
- The linear bound that the computed values suggest is still open (open problem 16).
- Theorem 20.6 rests on two cited theorems, not on a self-contained proof. Please check that both apply as stated: the image groups mod 3ᵏ (not SL₂(ℤ/3ᵏ) itself), and positive words.

## 6. SME-01: the world modulo 144

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, (ℤ/144)² ≅ (ℤ/16)² × (ℤ/9)², and the law acts on each factor separately.
- **Census.** The world modulo 16 has 16 orbits, of lengths 1, 3, 6 (two), 12 (four) and 24 (eight). The world modulo 9 has 5 orbits, of lengths 1, 8 and 24 (three).
- **Product rule.** A pair of orbits of lengths m and n splits into gcd(m, n) orbits of length lcm(m, n). So the world modulo 144 has **870 orbits**, of lengths 1, 3, 6 (two), 8, 12 (four) and 24 (861). This agrees with a direct count.
- **Spectrum.** On ℓ² of the 20,736 states, each 24th root of unity of order d has multiplicity equal to the number of orbits whose length d divides: 870, 868, 868, 866, 867, 862, 865 and 861 for d = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24.
- **Commutant dimensions:** 281 (modulo 9), 2,926 (modulo 16) and 17,916,106 (modulo 144).
- **The seed (2, 5)** has period 24 modulo 144.

**Implication for the historical 144.**
- Modulo 144, the 24-cycle is the typical orbit, not a rare one: 861 of 870 orbits have length 24.
- So 144 adds no new period. It adds a second, binary, factor whose orbits run in step with the ternary world. This is Companion 37.5 (2)'s finding in census form: every modulus from 12 upward has several period-24 orbits, none of which covers every residue.
- SME-01 is a correct and exact construction. It does not support the archive's statement that 144 is forced.
